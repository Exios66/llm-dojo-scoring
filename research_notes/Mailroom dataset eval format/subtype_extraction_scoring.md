# Per-Subtype Entity-Extraction Schemas and Their Scoring for Corporate Records

Research notes — October 2026. Scope: schema design (union vs per-subtype vs hierarchical) for corporate record subtypes (bylaws, charter/COI/formation, charter amendments, powers of attorney, subsidiary lists, rights instruments/certificates of designation, indentures, board resolutions, officer certificates), their canonical key entities, and scoring/provenance practice.

**Method note (important for the report writer):** the built-in web search integration in this session returned no results for any query, and the Firecrawl tooling was down (local instance refused connections). All findings below come from (a) direct fetches of primary sources (arXiv abstracts via the arXiv API, Delaware Code Online, Cornell LII CFR/US Code, and the inspect-ai repo docs), plus (b) long-standing background knowledge that could not be re-verified against live sources. Claims from un-verified background knowledge are flagged as such or moved to Gaps.

## Key Question 1: How do document-understanding benchmarks handle heterogeneous document classes — single union schema vs per-class schemas vs hierarchical (class → class-specific fields)? What's empirically better?

### Takeaway
There is no single head-to-head study comparing "one blanket schema" vs "per-class schemas" for LLM extraction evaluation; the dominant patterns in legal/document benchmarks are (a) a fixed union schema applied to every document (CUAD, ContractNLI, LexGLUE tasks) with "not applicable" as an implicit label value, or (b) evaluation organized **per class or per question with reference-dependent applicability** (MAUD, DocILE KILE, DUDE, Finance Agent Benchmark) — and published evidence suggests per-class/question structuring plus grounding verification is what modern evals converge toward.

### Cited Findings
- **CUAD (union schema with optional fields):** CUAD is "a new dataset for legal contract review... created with dozens of legal experts from The Atticus Project... over 13,000 annotations. The task is to highlight salient portions of a contract that are important for a human to review." CUAD's structure is a fixed set of ~41 clause "headers" asked of every contract, with a matching set of optional "sub-questions"/catch-all fields — i.e., a union schema where most clause categories are legitimately absent from a given contract. — [arXiv:2103.06268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06268)
- **LexGLUE (heterogeneous tasks normalized to one interface):** LexGLUE standardizes "a diverse set of legal NLU tasks" (multi-class classification, NLI, provision-head extraction from EDGAR filings via LEDGAR) into a single benchmark interface so models are compared across heterogeneous document types with a shared evaluation protocol (micro-F1). Task-level classes are per-dataset; there is no shared cross-document-type entity schema. — [arXiv:2110.00976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00976)
- **Performance is strongly task/class-dependent on union-style legal benchmarks:** a zero-shot audit of gpt-3.5-turbo on LexGLUE achieved an average micro-F1 of 47.6% but with large per-dataset variance (LEDGAR/E CtHR up to 62.8–70.2%) — evidence that a single aggregated score over heterogeneous document classes hides large per-class performance differences. — [arXiv:2304.12202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12202)
- **MAUD (hierarchical, per-clause applicability, expert labels):** MAUD is "an expert-annotated reading comprehension dataset based on the American Bar Association's 2021 Public Target Deal Points Study, with over 39,000 examples and over 47,000 total annotations." Its structure is a taxonomy of questions that apply conditionally to clauses actually present in each merger agreement — evaluation is scored per question, with "applicable answer set" handling, rather than one blanket field list. — [arXiv:2301.00876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00876)
- **DocILE (per-schema field extraction with localization):** DocILE is "the largest dataset of business documents for the tasks of Key Information Localization and Extraction and Line Item Recognition," containing "annotations in 55 classes, which surpasses the granularity of previously published key information extraction datasets," with zero- and few-shot document layout configurations in the test set. KILE evaluation in DocILE is scored per target field with localization (AP/F1 per class). — [arXiv:2302.05658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05658)
- **DUDE (class-aware, practically-oriented QA over visually-rich documents):** DUDE is a "multi-industry, multi-domain, and multi-page" DocAI benchmark with "multi-task and multi-domain evaluation setups," where question sets/answer types are tuned to document categories (invoices, forms, papers) — i.e., document classes drive which extraction targets are asked. — [arXiv:2305.08455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08455)
- **Heterogeneous schemas force complex workflows:** recent multimodal parsing work notes that "existing solutions often rely on task-specific architectures and objectives for individual tasks. This leads to modal isolation and complex workflows due to the diversified targets and heterogeneous schemas," motivating a schema-parameterized universal parser (per-schema configurations selected per document type). — [arXiv:2502.16161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16161)
- **Schema-aware caching for diverse schemas is now a recognized engineering pattern:** Dual-LoRA with Incremental Schema Caching (DLISC) addresses on-device LLM IE "particularly due to issues like hallucinations, limited context length, and high latency — especially when handling diverse extraction schemas," via a two-stage, schema-conditioned approach — evidence that the field treats per-schema conditioning as the practical answer to heterogeneous extraction targets. — [arXiv:2505.14992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14992)
- **EDGAR-based evals are organized as taxonomies, not blanket schemas:** the Finance Agent Benchmark evaluates "challenging and diverse real-world finance research problems" using "recent SEC filings" with "a taxonomy of nine financial task categories, developed in consultation with experts" — an explicit per-task-category structure. — [arXiv:2508.00828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00828) Capital markets reliability eval (CM-LRS) likewise argues open-domain QA benchmarks "evaluate at the question-answer layer rather than the workflow outputs practitioners defend," and scores structured drafting output against that workflow layer. — [arXiv:2607.21340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21340) Stanford EDGAR Filings Dataset reconstructs SEC filings "into layout-faithful MultiMarkdown" as substrate data for financial modeling/eval. — [arXiv:2606.18192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18192)

### Inferences
- For a multi-subtype corporate-records eval, the closest match to current best practice is a **two-level design**: (1) document-subtype classification, and (2) a subtype-conditioned schema (or LLM task configuration) per document class, with scoring reported per subtype and per field — mirroring how DocILE scores per field/class, MAUD per question, and Finance Agent Benchmark per task category.
- The union-schema design used by CUAD works because "absence" is a meaningful, annotated label set (most clause types are absent in any given contract); a blanket *key-entity* schema that forces every subtype through the same field list does not have that property — absence there is confounded with the document class, which is the core methodological objection to single-schema redesigns here.
- No controlled empirical study directly measuring extraction error rates under "blanket schema vs per-class schema" was found — this is an open gap below.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that directly ablates union vs per-class schemas for LLM entity extraction accuracy/precision (search covered arXiv API title/abstract queries for schema-pairing and evaluation studies; web search was nonfunctional).
- DocVQA-family ANLS (Average Normalized Levenshtein Similarity) practice could not be re-verified from a fetched source in this session.

## Key Question 2: Key entities per corporate record subtype, per real-world corporate practice (secretarial records, SEC filings)

### Takeaway
Authoritative state and federal sources enumerate the *statutorily required* "key entities" for charters, charter amendments, rights instruments, bylaws, indentures (trustee/officer-certificate machinery), and SEC exhibit-category declarations (subsidiary lists, powers of attorney, trustee eligibility statements). For board resolutions and officer certificates, the entity sets come from statute-procedural roles (Delaware governance provisions, TIA §314) and closing-document practice, not from a single statutory field list.

### Cited Findings
- **Certificate of incorporation (articles):** Delaware DGCL §102(a) enumerates the required contents: the corporate name (which "shall contain 1 of the words 'association,' 'company,' 'corporation,' ... 'limited'"); nature of business/purposes; the name of the registered agent and location of registered office in Delaware; the name and mailing address of the incorporator; and the authorized capital stock amounts and par value. Additional provisions (special rights, eliminations of personal liability, 102(b) provisions, closing-gap powers under §102(d)) are optional but standard. — [8 Del. C. §102, Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc01/index.html)
- **Rights instrument / preferred stock & certificate of designation:** DGCL §151(a): any class/series may have "such voting powers, full or limited, or no voting powers, and such designations, preferences and relative, participating, optional or other special rights, and qualifications, limitations or restrictions thereof, as shall be stated and expressed in the certificate of incorporation or of any amendment thereto, or **in the resolution or resolutions providing for the issue of such stock adopted by the board of directors pursuant to authority expressly vested in it by the provisions of its certificate of incorporation**"; such terms "may be made dependent upon facts ascertainable outside the certificate of incorporation." This single section supplies the key-entity set for rights instruments: series designation, voting powers, preferences/special rights and qualifications/limitations, plus the *board-authority delegation* linkage to the charter. — [8 Del. C. §151, Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc05/index.html)
- **Charter amendment:** DGCL §242(a) defines what an amendment may do (change corporate name; "change, substitute, enlarge or diminish the nature of its business or its corporate powers and purposes"; "increase or decrease its authorized capital stock or to reclassify the same, by changing the number, par value, designations, preferences, or relative, participating, optional, or other special rights of the shares..."), and how it must be effected (board adoption and stockholder approval mechanics described in that section, with different interplay for stock vs nonstock corporations). Amendment-relevant key entities per statute: target provision(s) amended, effect (e.g., authorized capital change), and adoption/approval pathway. — [8 Del. C. §242, Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc08/index.html)
- **Bylaws:** DGCL §109(a) enumerates the amendment-provision entities: bylaws may be adopted/amended/repealed by the incorporators, the initial directors (pre-payment), the board (before any payment for stock), post-payment by "the stockholders entitled to vote" (or members for nonstock corps), or — critically — "any corporation may, in its certificate of incorporation, confer the power to adopt, amend or repeal bylaws upon the directors" — and conferring that power on the board does not divest the stockholders of it. §109(b): the bylaws "may contain any provision, not inconsistent with law or with the certificate of incorporation, relating to... the management of the affairs of the corporation... the creation... of various offices." This gives the bylaws key-entity set: amendment power allocation (board vs stockholders vs fixed), governance/meeting provisions, and officer-creation provisions. — [8 Del. C. §109, Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc01/index.html)
- **Indenture (trustee machinery):** Trust Indenture Act §315 (15 U.S.C. §77ooo) makes the indenture itself the carrier of trustee duty provisions: pre-default, "the indenture trustee shall not be liable except for the performance of such duties as are specifically set out in such indenture" and may rely on "certificates or opinions conforming to the requirements of the indenture"; the trustee must "give to the indenture security holders... notice of all defaults known to the trustee, within ninety days after the occurrence thereof" (subject to good-faith withholding determinations); and in default must act "with... as a prudent man would exercise." — [15 U.S.C. §77ooo, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/77ooo)
- **Officer's certificate (as a legal instrument type):** TIA §314(c) (15 U.S.C. §77nnn(c)) defines what an officer's certificate *is and does*: the obligor must "furnish to the indenture trustee evidence of compliance with the conditions precedent... such as conditions with respect to the authentication and delivery of the indenture securities, to the release or the release and substitution of property..., to the satisfaction and discharge of the indenture," consisting of "**certificates or opinions made by officers of such obligor who are specified in the indenture, stating that such conditions precedent have been complied with**," plus an opinion of counsel and, for ratio-verifiable conditions ("preservation of specified ratios, the amount of net quick assets, negative-pledge clauses"), an accountant's certificate. Key entities: certifying officer (named/specified in indenture), capacity, conditions certified, date, and evidence set attached. — [15 U.S.C. §77nnn, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/77nnn)
- **Subsidiaries of the registrant** (subsidiary list as SEC record type): Item 601 of Regulation S-K (17 CFR 229.601) includes exhibit item "(21) Subsidiaries of the registrant" in the exhibit table. — [17 CFR §229.601, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/229.601)
- **Powers of attorney as a filing record type** and **trustee eligibility**: the same Item 601 exhibit table includes "(24) Power of attorney" and "(25) Statement of eligibility of trustee" — i.e., PoA (signing authority PoAs accompanying filings) and the trustee-eligibility statement (indenture-side record) are recognized SEC exhibit record subtypes in their own right. — [17 CFR §229.601, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/229.601)
- **Indentures as "rights of security holders" instruments:** Item 601(a)(4) also governs filing of "a material contract... executed or becomes effective during the reporting period" and the exhibit table separately caters to indentures/instruments defining security-holder rights (the (b)(4) text itself was not captured in the fetch; see Gap). — [17 CFR §229.601, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/229.601)

### Inferences (clearly marked — practice, not verified statute text)
- **Indenture key entities** beyond TIA-named items (trustee identity, duties, default-notice regime): coupon/interest rate, maturity, redemption provisions, and covenants are standard practitioner-facing indenture terms, but this session did not fetch a source that enumerates them (the classical enumeration lives in practitioner forms such as model indentures and Exhibit-list material contracts; candidates: TIA §310–316 architecture, Item 601(b)(4)).
- **Board resolution key entities** (approving body — board vs committee; resolution number; meeting/adoption date; effective date; delegated authority source; signatories/attest) are derivable from DGCL §151(a)'s "resolution or resolutions providing for the issue of such stock adopted by the board of directors pursuant to authority expressly vested in it," and from §109's amendment-power language — but no fetched statute enumerates resolution-form fields like resolution numbers (those are secretarial practice/convention).
- **Bylaws quorum/voting thresholds and officer slate** (quorum/vote thresholds, officer offices named, indemnification) are standard bylaws content per §109(b)'s open-ended authorization; DGCL §§142/211/102 patterns underpin indemnification (§102(b)(7) exculpation/indemnification option in the certificate) — not fetched this session, flagged under Gaps.
- **Conceptual takeaway for schema design:** the statutory "key entities" are exactly the *decision-relevant provisions* a records-reviewer must see (who has power, what was done, whether procedure was satisfied) — a defensible organizing principle for per-subtype field lists.

### Gaps
- No fetched source enumerates the complete practitioner key-entity list per subtype (e.g., coupon/maturity/covenants fields for indentures; quorum thresholds and officer slates in bylaws; resolution numbering conventions for board resolutions). SEC Item 601(b)(4) and (b)(21) full instruction text (e.g., the requirement that the (21) exhibit list all significant subsidiaries with jurisdiction/ownership detail) could not be captured from the Cornell LII fetch (page truncated at the exhibit table). Model charter documents / law-firm annotated forms (e.g., Wilson Sonsini model bylaws, Davis Polk annotated indenture) were not accessible without working web search.
- Powers of attorney as *secretarial records* (vs exhibit filings) have no statute-specified field list found this session.

## Key Question 3: Scoring designs for entity extraction — exact vs fuzzy vs containment; per-field presence vs field-conditional value scoring; scoring "not applicable" fields; how established scorers implement field-weighted/hierarchical scoring

### Takeaway
Established eval frameworks (inspect-ai) support graded, field-weighted, partially-credited scoring natively (Score values may be "C"/"I" **or any numeric value**; multiple scorers can be emitted and reduced), and modern extraction evals increasingly score *groundedness/provenance* (SafePassage) rather than only string match. No framework found prescribes a canonical rule for "ground truth = not applicable" fields — that must be designed locally.

### Cited Findings
- **inspect-ai scorer taxonomy:** scorers "evaluate whether solvers were successful" in "one of the following forms: (1) extracting a specific answer... using a variety of heuristics; (2) applying a text similarity algorithm to see if the model's completion is close to what is set out in the target; (3) using another model to assess whether the completion satisfies a description of the ideal answer; (4) using another rubric entirely." — [inspect_ai docs/scorers.qmd](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai/blob/main/docs/scorers.qmd)
- **Partial credit is first-class in inspect-ai:** a `Score`'s `value` can be `"C"`, `"I"`, **"or a raw numeric value"** (e.g., `Score(value=0.6)`), with `answer`, `explanation`, and free-form `metadata` for logging per-field detail; custom scorers may register custom metrics (e.g., a tolerance-matched "close_enough" fuzzy scorer in the documented example). — [inspect_ai docs/custom-scorers.qmd](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai/blob/main/docs/custom-scorers.qmd)
- **Multiple scores per sample / reduction:** the docs point to a "Multiple Scorers" workflow "use several scorers together, emit multiple scores, and reduce them" — the natural substrate for per-field vs aggregate (field-weighted) reporting. — [inspect_ai docs/scorers.qmd](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai/blob/main/docs/scorers.qmd)
- **Groundedness/provenance scoring:** SafePassage addresses exactly the central extraction-eval trust problem: with black-box LLM extraction "the information 'extracted' is not guaranteed to be grounded in the document," and it operationalizes "safe passage" via a three-step pipeline (extract structured entities *with contexts* → verify context is grounded and consistent with the extraction → use a "judge" step). This is an existence proof of extraction scoring where per-entity provenance (supporting span) is part of the scored output. — [arXiv:2510.00276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00276)
- **Aspect-decomposed scoring under human/IAA constraints:** T5Score "decomposes the quality of a topic set into quantifiable aspects, measurable through easy-to-perform annotation tasks" precisely because existing eval practices "result in low inter-annotator agreement scores" — supporting the practice of scoring multi-field outputs as a set of separately-annotatable aspects rather than one blob. — [arXiv:2407.17390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17390)
- **Field/aspect-granular gold standard design:** "Revisiting the Gold Standard" introduced "Atomic Content Units (ACUs), which is based on fine-grained semantic units and allows for a high inter-annotator agreement" — an argument that finer-grained, per-unit (≈per-field) scoring is *more*, not less, reliable than coarse scoring. — [arXiv:2212.07981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07981)
- **No-gold-standard comparisons:** GEM provides an evaluation method for LLM-generated judgments "without the need for a gold standard reference" (generative mutual-information estimation) — relevant if subtype field values drift domain-specifically and full adjudication is cost-prohibitive. — [arXiv:2411.07127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07127)

### Inferences (method-design synthesis for the redesign)
- A per-subtype schema plus **per-field scoring** decomposes cleanly onto inspect-ai primitives: register one scorer per subtype, emit per-field `Score`s (presence + value-accuracy), reduce with field-weighted metrics, and log provenance in Score `metadata`/`explanation` so adjudication and debugging stay possible.
- Recommended three-tier field scoring (grounded in findings above, marked inference): (1) **field presence/agreement** (was the field correctly populated or correctly left absent/NA), (2) **value accuracy** conditional on presence (exact match for closed-vocabulary/number fields; normalized similarity or containment for free text/dates), and (3) **provenance/guardrail checks** (per SafePassage: does the model supply a supporting passage for each populated entity).
- For "ground truth = not applicable" fields: no cited framework prescribes a rule; the defensible design (inference from CUAD's absent-clause labeling and MAUD's per-question applicability structure) is to score "not applicable" as its own correctness dimension — the model gets credit for leaving the field empty/NA and is penalized (typically most heavily) for populating a field that is not applicable — i.e., presence is scored symmetrically with value.
- Exact vs fuzzy vs containment: recommend exact for lexical/numeric/bounded enums (dates, names, quorums), sub-theme similarity or normalized partial-credit for long-form provision text — matching inspect-ai's documented "close_enough" tolerance pattern for numerics and its text-similarity scorer class.

### Gaps
- No primary source found prescribing "field-weighted hierarchical scoring" templates for IE; the lm-eval-harness family of metrics (exact match, loglikelihood-based) could not be re-fetched this session (repo/docs fetch not performed; leaving lm-eval specifics uncited).
- No published empirical comparison of exact-match vs fuzzy/ANLS scoring distortion for legal field values was found.

## Key Question 4: Label provenance and adjudication — how benchmarks record heuristic/LLM/human labels, and thresholds before heuristic labels are trusted

### Takeaway
High-quality legal benchmarks make expert/human provenance a headline design property (CUAD's "dozens of legal experts," MAUD's "expert-annotated," LegalBench's professionally hand-crafted tasks), while the newer methodological literature warns that low inter-annotator agreement is endemic and shows that fine-grained units and decomposed aspects *restore* IAA — but no standardized numeric threshold convention (e.g., "you may trust heuristic labels above kappa X") was found.

### Cited Findings
- CUAD labels were "created with dozens of legal experts from The Atticus Project" (13,000+ annotations). — [arXiv:2103.06268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06268)
- MAUD is "an **expert-annotated** reading comprehension dataset based on the American Bar Association's 2021 Public Target Deal Points Study, with over 39,000 examples and over 47,000 total annotations" — professional-judgment provenance is the dataset's core claim. — [arXiv:2301.00876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00876)
- LegalBench tasks were "designed and hand-crafted by legal professionals"; its construction "in which we collected tasks designed and hand-crafted by legal professionals" — provenance recorded at the task level. — [arXiv:2308.11462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11462)
- Trade-off evidence for expert provenance: "Many specialized domains remain untouched by deep learning, as large labeled datasets require expensive expert annotators" (CUAD's motivating bottleneck) — i.e., the cost that pushes benchmarks toward heuristic/LLM pre-labeling. — [arXiv:2103.06268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06268)
- IAA fragility: "existing evaluation practices... result in low inter-annotator agreement scores" (T5Score, on LLM-generated topic sets) and "existing human evaluation studies for summarization either exhibit a low inter-annotator agreement or have insufficient scale" (Revisiting the Gold Standard) — both motivation for explicit IAA checks on any auto/heuristic labels. — [arXiv:2407.17390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17390); [arXiv:2212.07981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07981)
- Both papers adduce remedies tied to fine-grained structure: T5Score's aspect decomposition is designed to make agreement "measurable through easy-to-perform annotation tasks"; Revisiting the Gold Standard's Atomic Content Units "allows for a high inter-annotator agreement." This directly supports recording *per-field* provenance and adjudication in a per-subtype schema design. — [arXiv:2407.17390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17390); [arXiv:2212.07981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07981)
- Where gold labels are absent, GEM ("Benchmarking LLMs' Judgments with No Gold Standard") offers mutual-information-based comparison without a reference — an option when heuristic labels cannot yet be trusted. — [arXiv:2411.07127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07127)

### Inferences
- For the redesign, provenance should be a **per-field (not per-document) attribute** in the schema: `{origin: human | heuristic | llm, adjudicated: bool, evidence_span}` — consistent with the per-unit design logic in T5Score/ACU findings.
- Because no threshold convention was found in citable sources, recommend stating spot-check/IAA numbers explicitly in the eval design (e.g., dual-annotate a fixed fraction of fields across subtypes and report agreement per subtype), rather than invoking a standard "≥0.8" rule as if it were a settled requirement. (Note: an IAA ≥ 0.8 convention exists in the annotation literature (Krippendorff/Artstein-Poesio tradition), but it was not verified from a fetched source in this session.)

### Gaps
- No source was found that establishes a specific spot-check rate or IAA threshold for *trusting heuristic/LLM pre-labels in benchmark gold sets*; the weak-supervision-in-legal-IE literature found (e.g., arXiv:2305.05836, complex legal NER via weak supervision; arXiv:2501.14114, weakly supervised legal concepts) treats weak labels as training signals, not as scoring gold, which itself is evidence that the field does *not* advocate trusting heuristic labels unsupervised for gold scoring — but a definitive threshold convention could not be cited. — [arXiv:2305.05836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05836); [arXiv:2501.14114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14114)
- The Data Provenance Initiative (Longpre et al.) is a known general provenance-auditing effort for datasets (dataprovenance.org), but its paper could not be retrieved/verified this session (arXiv search returned unrelated papers), so no details are asserted here.

## Key Question 5: Known failure modes when one schema is blanket-applied across record subtypes (precision collapse, hallucinated inapplicable fields, penalizing legitimately absent fields)

### Takeaway
Direct empirical postmortems of "one blanket schema across document subtypes" were not found; however, multiple 2025–2026 papers independently document the constituent failure modes that a blanket schema invites — ungrounded (hallucinated) extraction values, degradation when handling diverse schemas in one configuration, and benchmark aggregates that mask per-class差异 — the pieces that in combination justify per-subtype schemas.

### Cited Findings
- **Ungrounded values are the default risk:** "Black box large language models (LLMs) make information extraction (IE) easy to configure, but hard to trust. Unlike traditional information extraction pipelines, the information 'extracted' is not guaranteed to be grounded in the document." SafePassage thus adds an explicit grounding-verification step over structured entities + contexts. For a records eval, any field schema (blanket or per-subtype) inherits this risk, but it is amplified when the model is asked for fields that don't exist in the document (no span can ground them). — [arXiv:2510.00276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00276)
- **Handling "diverse extraction schemas" is itself a documented pain point:** DLISC is motivated by LLM IE problems "particularly due to issues like hallucinations, limited context length, and high latency — especially when handling **diverse extraction schemas**," and proposes two-stage schema-cached extraction as the mitigation — i.e., the expert pattern is: don't throw all schemas at the model at once; condition the extraction pass. — [arXiv:2505.14992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14992)
- **Heterogeneous targets → complex workflows:** OmniParser V2's diagnosis that "existing solutions often rely on task-specific architectures and objectives for individual tasks... complex workflows due to the diversified targets and heterogeneous schemas" corroborates that heterogeneous field sets per document class are the actual production problem, not an artifact. — [arXiv:2502.16161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16161)
- **Aggregate scores mask per-class collapse:** the LexGLUE audit showed a mean micro-F1 of 47.6% with individual legal NLU datasets ranging to 62.8–70.2% for the model's strong datasets — direct evidence that aggregating heterogeneous record types into one number hides where precision collapses. — [arXiv:2304.12202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12202)
- **Workshop-layer vs surface accuracy:** CM-LRS critiques that existing finance/legal benchmarks "reward surface accuracy" and "evaluate at the question-answer layer rather than the workflow outputs practitioners defend" — implying precision failures (plausible-but-unbankable extractions) pass QA-layer union benchmarks. — [arXiv:2607.21340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21340)
- **Union schemas with annotated-absence (CUAD) are the exception:** CUAD's own results note that performance is "strongly influenced by" [continuation not captured in fetch — sentence truncated at "influenced by"], consistent with its known finding that clause-header extraction depends materially on the rarity of clause types in the corpus (rare clause types are the hard classes) — with the specific sentence unverified here. — [arXiv:2103.06268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06268)

### Inferences
- The three blanket-schema failure modes posited in the task (precision collapse via irrelevant fields, hallucinated inapplicable fields, penalizing legitimately absent fields) each have supporting-but-indirect evidence: (a) per-class variance on kernel legal benchmarks (2304.12202), (b) ungrounded extraction risk (2510.00276) + schema-diversity pain (2505.14992), (c) symmetric-presence scoring design (inference from CUAD/MAUD structure). None has a direct named empirical study on corporate-records subtypes; all quantified failure-mode numbers must come from the redesign's own ablation.
- For a mailroom corporate-records eval, the redesign that follows from the cited literature is: per-subtype schemas selected by document class (DocILE/DLISC/OmniParser pattern); per-field presence+value+provenance scoring with numeric partial credit recorded in inspect-ai-style structured scores; and per-subtype (not blanket) metric reporting so aggregates can't mask collapse (LexGLUE-audit lesson).

### Gaps
- No empirical postmortem or ablation study specifically titled around "union schema vs per-class schema for LLM extraction" was found (arXiv-focused search; public web search was unavailable in this session). Any 2026 practitioner blog/industry postmortem evidence would need a separate search pass.
- The precise CUAD performance-determinant sentence (and any clause-type rarity statistics) was truncated in the fetched abstract and left unverified.
- Item 601(b)(4)/(b)(21) full instruction text and any 2024–2026 amendments to the Exhibit list could not be pulled (eCFR blocked automated access; Cornell LII fetch truncated before paragraph (b) enumerations).
